## Product Overview
**One-liner:** The streaming platform built exclusively for AI-generated films — watch them, or publish your own.

**What it does:** YouMakeTV is a two-sided marketplace for AI-generated cinema. Viewers browse a curated catalog of ~100 AI-made films, watch free titles, and buy or rent paid ones. Creators who make films with tools like Veo, Runway, Sora, Kling and Midjourney publish them to a distribution channel built around AI filmmaking rather than one that treats it as an afterthought.

**Product category:** AI-native streaming platform / film marketplace. Customers looking for this search "where to watch AI-generated movies" or "where to publish my AI film."

**Product type:** Two-sided marketplace with a subscription layer (B2C both sides).

**Business model:**
- Transactional: films priced free–$4.99 (catalog average $4.24). Platform takes a fee on paid views.
- Subscription: **YouMake+** — **$4.99/month**. There is no annual plan on sale; monthly is the only term offered today. Benefits: unlimited free-tier streaming, 50% off every paid film, member pricing.
- Creator revenue share: 30% standard, up to 40% for the Pro tier. Free to join and publish.
- *Status: transactional and subscription billing are both **not yet connected**.*

## Target Audience
**Target companies:** Not applicable — this is B2C on both sides. The "creator" side skews toward solo creators and 1–3 person studios rather than production companies.

**Decision-makers:** The individual — no committee, no procurement.

**Primary use case:**
- *Viewers:* find AI-generated films worth watching, in one place, without sifting through social feeds.
- *Creators:* get AI films in front of an audience that actually wants them, and eventually earn from that.

**Jobs to be done:**
- "Show me what AI filmmaking can actually do, as finished films — not clips and demos."
- "Give my AI film a real home with a price on it, not just a post that disappears in a day."
- "Let me watch AI cinema without paying for a subscription per film I try."

**Use cases:**
- Curious viewer discovering the medium for the first time via free titles.
- Enthusiast following specific AI studios and buying new releases.
- AI filmmaker publishing a first short and building a studio page as a portfolio.
- Creator experimenting with pricing — free to build an audience, paid once there is demand.

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** AI filmmaking has outrun its distribution. People are making genuinely watchable films with AI tools, and there is nowhere purpose-built to release them — so the work lands on general platforms where it competes with everything else and is often penalized for being AI-made.

**Why alternatives fall short:**
- **YouTube / TikTok / Instagram** — built for feeds, not films. No purchase model, AI content increasingly deprioritized or labelled, no way to charge for a 90-minute piece.
- **Vimeo On Demand / Prime Video Direct** — will host the file, but the audience is not looking for AI work and the storefront does not signal it.
- **Film festivals and AI showcases** — episodic, curated, gatekept. Great if selected, nothing if not.
- **Personal sites and Patreon** — you own it, but you also have to bring the entire audience yourself.

**What it costs them:** Weeks or months of production work reaching a few hundred views and no revenue. For viewers: no reliable way to find work worth their time, so they sample a few clips and conclude the medium is not ready.

**Emotional tension:** Creators feel dismissed — that "AI-generated" is treated as a disqualifier rather than a technique. Viewers feel slightly embarrassed to be curious about it, and burned by clips that oversell.

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** No established AI-only film marketplace at scale — the category is still forming. This is a land-grab position, not a displacement play.
**Direct-adjacent:** Curious Refuge, AI film festival circuits (Runway's AIFF) — fall short because they are events and communities, not always-on storefronts with a price attached.
**Secondary:** YouTube, Vimeo On Demand, Prime Video Direct — fall short because AI work is not what their audience arrives looking for, and their economics assume ads or scale.
**Indirect:** Netflix and conventional streaming — falls short because the whole appeal here is seeing what the new tools produce, which traditional studios do not offer.
**Indirect:** Doing nothing — posting to X/Discord for free. Falls short because it never becomes a business.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- Exclusively AI-generated films — the constraint *is* the product. No competing with conventional content for attention.
- AI tools used are surfaced on every film as first-class metadata (Veo, Runway, Sora, Kling, Midjourney, ElevenLabs, Stable Diffusion, DALL·E).
- Creators keep full IP ownership; YouMakeTV distributes.
- Creators set their own price, including free, and can change it anytime.
- Studio pages give small creators a professional storefront.
- Viewer-side subscription (YouMake+) lowers the cost of sampling the medium, which is the real barrier to adoption.

**How we do it differently:** Rather than tolerating AI content, the entire catalog, discovery model and creator workflow assume it. A viewer arriving here has already opted into AI cinema, so creators are not fighting for context.

**Why that is better:** Intent-matched audience. A film gets in front of people who came specifically to watch AI films.

**Why customers choose us:** Viewers — it is the only curated place to see finished AI films rather than clips. Creators — it is the only shelf where "AI-generated" is the point rather than a disclaimer.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "AI films aren't good enough to pay for" | Most of the catalog is free to watch. Sample first, buy only what earns it. |
| "Who owns my film if I upload it?" | You do. Creators retain all IP; YouMakeTV holds distribution rights only, and you can unpublish anytime. |
| "How big is the audience really?" | Be honest: the platform is early and building. Do not quote traffic figures — see Honesty Guardrails. |
| "When do I actually get paid?" | Be honest: billing is not connected yet. Revenue share terms are set (30%, up to 40%), but no payouts have been made and we will email every creator before real payments begin. |
| "Isn't this just YouTube with extra steps?" | YouTube cannot charge $2.99 for your film, does not signal AI work to an audience that wants it, and does not give you a studio page. |

**Anti-persona:**
- Live-action or conventional filmmakers — the catalog is AI-only, they would be out of place.
- Creators who need income now. Payouts are not live; anyone joining for immediate revenue will be disappointed and should be told so up front.
- Viewers wanting mainstream studio content — wrong shelf entirely.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** AI work getting buried, demonetized or labelled on general platforms; no way to charge for it; audiences who do not care about the craft.
**Pull:** A dedicated shelf, a price tag, full ownership, an audience that arrived for exactly this, and a studio page that looks legitimate.
**Habit:** Posting to YouTube/X out of reflex because that is where the follower count already is.
**Anxiety:** "Is anyone actually there?" (early-stage audience), "Will I really get paid?" (billing not live), "Do I lose rights by uploading?" (no).

## Goals
**Business goal:** Build supply and audience simultaneously in a category that does not have an incumbent — get real creators publishing real films, and get viewers into the habit of watching AI cinema in one place.
**Conversion action:**
- *Primary (creator side):* start creator onboarding at `/creator/onboarding`.
- *Primary (viewer side):* watch a free film, then YouMake+ signup.
- *Secondary:* contact form submission — already routes to the Growth OS CRM (source `youmaketv-contact`).
**Current metrics:** Unknown and largely unmeasured. Event tracking (`/api/track`) is live and records trailer plays, purchases, signups, subscriptions and movie views, but has no meaningful history yet.

## Honesty Guardrails
*These override anything above. The platform is mid-build and public — copy must not outrun it.*

1. **Never promise payouts.** No "get paid monthly," no "start earning today," no payout schedule stated as operating fact. Revenue share *terms* may be described as terms. Payment processing is not connected and no creator has been paid.
2. **Never invent proof.** No earnings figures, creator counts, view counts, subscriber numbers, testimonials or "trusted by" claims. None of these exist. Catalog view counts are seeded data, not traffic.
3. **Keep the beta notices.** `src/components/BetaNotice.tsx` is deliberate and states billing is not connected. It must stay wherever a dollar figure appears, and its meaning must not be softened.
4. **Label illustrative UI.** Dashboard previews and sample figures on marketing pages must be visibly marked as examples, never implied to be live data.
5. **Subscriptions are not selling.** YouMake+ pricing is configured but not billing. Describe it as coming, not as available.
6. **When in doubt, say less.** An honest, smaller claim is worth more than a claim that has to be walked back to real creators.
